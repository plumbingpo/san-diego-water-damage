Wall and Drywall Water Damage Help in San Diego

Think of drywall as a wick. When water reaches the bottom of a wall, the gypsum and paper draw it upward, often a foot or more above the level the water ever reached on the floor. Insulation and the rear of the panel can be wet through while the paint out front shows nothing unusual. For that reason our team relies on moisture meter readings rather than appearance.

Plenty of walls that get wet can stay. Drywall that got wet with clean water and is still firm can often be dried in place, sometimes by removing the baseboard and moving air through small holes into the cavity. When the wall is soft, crumbling, moldy or was soaked by dirty water, we make a flood cut, a straight line above the damage, and remove everything below it.

Signs to Look For

  • Paint or wallpaper that bubbles, wrinkles or peels near the floor
  • Drywall seams ridging up and screw pops appearing across the surface
  • Speckled growth showing up where furniture sits against the wall or at corners
  • New gaps along casings and baseboard tops as swollen material shifts
  • Soft or crumbly spots you notice when pushing lightly on the wall
  • Cool, slightly clammy drywall next to walls at normal temperature

Our Process

Our Wall and Drywall Water Damage Process

1

Find Every Wet Area

Using meter readings and thermal images, our team outlines where the water went, upward and sideways, on both faces of the wall and in neighboring rooms.

2

Decide What Stays

You get a straight answer on what can be dried where it is and what must be removed, judged by the category of water, the time it sat and the firmness of the drywall.

3

Cut or Ventilate

Where the drywall stays, the trim is pulled and airflow is sent inside the wall. On sections coming out, a straight flood cut is made and soaked insulation is removed.

4

Drying and Reconstruction

Drying typically takes 3 to 5 days of air movement and dehumidification before the framing and surviving drywall meter dry. After that comes insulation, new drywall hung and finished, trim put back and paint.

The Clock

What Happens if You Wait

The first few hours

Water Starts Climbing

The bottom edge of the drywall takes in water from the floor and it begins moving up. Both the baseboard and the bottom plate start absorbing water.

Hours 24 to 48

Wall Swells

You may see blistered paint and swollen trim as the damp area moves up the wall. Inside the cavity, the paper face becomes a place mold can begin.

Days 3 through 7

Panels Lose Strength

The gypsum core turns soft and its paper starts to separate. Insulation slumps inside the wall, odor builds and mold spreads across the hidden back side.

Past one week

Damage Reaches the Studs

By this point the studs and plates have been damp long enough for mold and early rot. Much more wall now has to be removed and rebuilt.

Behind the Surface

Risks That Are Easy to Miss

Wet Sheathing Outside

Water in a perimeter wall may reach the sheathing on the far side of the cavity. That layer dries slowly and is checked before the wall is closed.

Electrical Hazards Low Down

Receptacles and cables sit low in the wall, right where water collects. Until that circuit is inspected, avoid plugging anything into the affected wall.

Insulation Holding Water

Batts of fiberglass absorb water and keep it pressed to the framing. A wall with wet insulation will not dry from the room side alone.

Growth Behind the Paint

The back of the drywall is dark, still and lined with paper, which is ideal for mold. You can have a clean looking painted surface with heavy growth right behind it.
