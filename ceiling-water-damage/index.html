Ceiling Water Damage Repair in San Diego

The stain overhead comes at the end of the story, not the beginning. By the time the stain appears, water has already run along a joist, soaked the insulation and pooled on the back of the drywall. The leak may be a bathroom on the floor above, a pipe in the ceiling, an air conditioner drain line or the roof. Nothing is repaired until the origin of the water has been tracked down.

Painting over the stain does not fix it. While the space overhead stays damp, the stain returns, and within 24 to 48 hours mold can begin on the drywall paper. Our crew removes no more ceiling than necessary, takes out soaked insulation, dries the framing and the drywall that stays, and finishes with a patch, texture and paint that match. A sagging ceiling is a different matter. Keep clear of that area and phone us.

Warning Signs

  • Hairline cracks spreading outward from a stained or damp spot
  • Tan or rust colored circles that widen following a storm or a shower
  • Small black or green specks appearing in a cluster overhead
  • Stains or streaks running down the wall just below the ceiling line
  • Ceiling paint lifting off in blisters or peeling strips
  • Drywall tape lifting and seams or screw heads starting to show
How We Fix It

Our Ceiling Water Damage Repair Process

1

Trace the Leak

The first job is locating the starting point, which involves meter readings, a thermal camera and a look at whatever is over the ceiling. Until the water stops coming, fixing the ceiling would not last.

2

Secure the Room

We cut power to any wet fixture and move or cover the furniture underneath. A ceiling with pooled water is relieved carefully, with the water collected as it comes out.

3

Open and Dry

Damaged drywall and wet insulation come out. The joists and nearby ceiling are dried with air movers plus a dehumidifier, and antimicrobial goes on wherever the water was dirty.

4

Rebuild and Paint

After the meter shows normal levels, new insulation and drywall go up, joints are taped and finished, the texture is matched and a stain blocking primer goes on ahead of paint.

Behind the Surface

The Damage You Do Not See

Wet Lights and Wiring

The damp area usually includes fan mounts, recessed fixtures and wiring boxes. A short is possible when water enters a light, which is why the breaker should be shut off.

Mold Above You

Mold usually grows out of sight on the upper face of the panel and in the insulation above, both of which hold moisture well after the painted side dries.

What the Upstairs Is Hiding

The structure on the upper side, attic boards or the floor of the next story, tends to be wet too. Leaving it out of the drying plan lets dampness linger, so both levels get inspected.

Older Ceiling Materials

Some older popcorn textures and plasters contain asbestos. If your home has one, it should be tested before anyone scrapes, cuts or tears out a damaged area.

If Nothing Is Done

What Happens if You Wait

First hours

Hidden Travel Overhead

The insulation gets wet as water travels down the framing. Because the panel soaks from its top side, nothing may look wrong from underneath.

Hours 24 to 48

Marks Start Showing

Discoloration shows up and the paint begins bubbling. Soaked insulation weighs on the drywall while mold can start on its paper face.

Days 3 through 7

The Ceiling Bows

Drywall softens and pulls away from its screws. The ceiling bows between joists, seams open up and a musty odor reaches the room.

After a week

Risk of Collapse

Saturated pieces may drop and carry soaked insulation down too. The repair zone is far bigger at this stage, with mold well rooted inside the cavity.
