Why Sewage Backups Happen in San Diego, CA

Home / Blog / Preventing Sewage Backups in San Diego, CA

A sewage backup is one of the most unpleasant emergencies a homeowner can face. The result is extensive damage, an immediate threat to health, and a professional cleanup bill that can reach thousands of dollars whenever raw sewage enters the rooms you live in. Understanding why sewage backups occur and taking preventive measures can significantly reduce your risk. Below, San Diego Restoration Co explains what usually causes them for San Diego, CA homeowners, plus the practical things you can do to protect your home.

Why Sewage Backups Happen

Sewage backups occur when wastewater cannot flow from your property to the municipal sewer system or septic tank as intended. The most common causes include: clogged sewer lines from grease, hair, and debris buildup; tree root intrusion into sewer pipes; aging or collapsed sewer pipes; municipal sewer system overloads during heavy rainfall; combined sewer overflow events; and improper items flushed down toilets including wipes, feminine products, and excessive paper.

Tree Roots in the Sewer Line

Tree roots are one of the most common causes of sewer line blockages in San Diego. Because roots grow toward water, a pipe that always carries it draws them in. Roots can infiltrate pipes through joints, cracks, and connection points, gradually filling the pipe interior and causing blockages that lead to backups. Older clay tile sewer pipes are particularly vulnerable to root intrusion. Having your sewer line inspected with a camera every 2 to 3 years can identify root problems before they cause a backup.

How to Prevent a Sewage Backup

Several habits and upgrades help keep sewage out of a San Diego home, and the first is a backwater valve on the sewer connection, which stops backflow from the municipal system. Never pour grease or cooking oil down drains. Do not flush feminine products, wipes, or anything other than toilet paper. Routine inspection and cleaning of your sewer line should be on the calendar. Address slow drains promptly before they become complete blockages. Know the location of your sewer cleanout for emergency access. Think about adding a sewage backup endorsement to your homeowner's policy, or buying sewer line insurance.

What to Do If Sewage Backs Up

When sewage backs up, leave the cleanup alone. It carries viruses, bacteria, parasites, and other dangerous pathogens capable of making you seriously ill.
