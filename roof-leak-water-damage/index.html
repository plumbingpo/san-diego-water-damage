Dealing With Roof Leak Water Damage in San Diego

Where you see the stain and where the rain gets in are usually two different places. A loose shingle, a failed flashing joint or an aging seal around a skylight lets rain in, and it travels across the roof deck and framing until it falls into the insulation. A ceiling stain tends to arrive late, well after the space overhead first got wet. Our team maps everything that got wet, then dries it.

To be clear, roof repair is not what we do, water damage is. We work on the interior, which means removing wet insulation, drying drywall and framing, dealing with mold and rebuilding the ceiling. If rain is still coming in, we can tarp the roof as a temporary cover, or work alongside the roofer you choose so the leak is fixed before we close anything up.

Do This First

Your First Steps

  1. Cut Power to Wet FixturesWater coming through a ceiling fan, a light or close to an outlet means that circuit should be turned off at the panel, and the fixture should not be touched.
  2. Contain the WaterSet buckets or pans under active drips and move furniture, rugs and electronics out of the way. Plastic or towels spread on the floor will keep it from soaking.
  3. Drain a Bulging CeilingWhen water pools in a bubble overhead, pierce the low point with a screwdriver while standing clear, and let it run into a bucket.
  4. Leave the Roof AloneA wet roof and an unlit attic are both places to stay out of. Just observe the entry point from the room below, then hand the job to a trained crew.
  5. Photograph the DamageUse your phone to record the damaged ceiling, attic and belongings first, hold off on throwing things out until that is done, and call us to get the drying started.
How We Fix It

How We Handle Roof Leak Water Damage

1

Trace the Leak Path

Using thermal imaging and moisture meters, our team checks wall cavities, ceilings and the attic and charts the whole route the water took, even where the room below shows nothing.

2

Tarp and Protect

If the roof is still open to the weather, we can install a temporary tarp or coordinate with your roofer. Anything sitting beneath the damage, including the flooring, gets protected too.

3

Tear Out and Dry

Unsalvageable drywall and insulation come out, and dehumidifiers and air movers go into the attic space and each room under it, with readings taken until the framing has dried.

4

Treat and Rebuild

Exposed wood gets an antimicrobial treatment where needed. After the roofer has finished, new insulation goes in, fresh drywall is hung and finished, and the ceiling is primed and painted to match.

Insurance

What About Insurance?

When a leak happens suddenly and by accident, for example a branch going through the roof or wind stripping shingles, many homeowners policies pay for the water damage inside. Insurers generally turn down claims tied to wear, age or lack of upkeep, and they may handle the roof repair on different terms than the inside damage. Photos and moisture readings from us go to the adjuster, though only your policy decides coverage, and your agent can explain it.

The Usual Culprits

What Causes It

  • Rubber boots around plumbing vent pipes that split after years in the sun.
  • Nails that have backed out of the decking and left small holes through the shingles.
  • Shingles that are cracked, curled or blown off by wind, exposing the underlayment.
  • Storm impact, such as hail or a tree branch, breaking through the shingles.
  • Clogged gutters that back water up under the shingles at the roof edge.
If It Is Left

What the Water Damages

Ceiling Fixtures and Wiring

Recessed cans, ceiling fans and electrical boxes are low points where roof water collects and drips through. Wet connections can short or corrode, so the circuit should stay off until it is checked.

Ceiling Drywall

A ceiling panel left damp will discolor, bow and weaken. The tape releases along cracked seams, and with enough weight a section can come down suddenly.

Roof Framing

Rafters, trusses and decking that get wet again and again begin to darken, swell and soften. Once they rot, the frame supporting the roof is weaker.

Insulation in the Attic

Once soaked, insulation flattens, loses its value and keeps the drywall under it damp for days. Drying is sometimes possible with fiberglass. Soaked cellulose usually has to be removed.
